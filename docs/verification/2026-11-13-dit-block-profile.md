## 1. What ran

  block NEFFs (model code on every rank, confirmed by a per-model signature in `signatures.json`)

  `system_profile.py` gives each block NEFF's executions per step and in-model median. Every

  LTX-2 and Wan compile a different block NEFF on every rank (ruling R21), and only rank 0's is

- **Analysis** (`analyze.py <blk> --model <key>` -> `breakdown.json`), CPU only: busy time per
  engine and per component, attention phases, PSUM read-out, unattributed time by source, the
  time line. **Collection** (`collect.py`): joins breakdowns and system summaries on the NEFF name,
  adds the step times and quality rows of `docs/verification/2026-11-07-mxfp8-video-models.md`
  (sections 7f, 10, 11, 12; host B is the second trn3 of section 12), and writes `results.json`,
  the page's datasets and these tables. Nothing was rerun for step times or quality.

## 2. Definitions

- **Engine time** = busy time of one physical core: the union of its instruction intervals
  (overlapping pipelined instructions count once), `EVENT_SEMAPHORE*` waits excluded, then the mean
  over the two physical cores of the logical core. It reproduces the profile's `ActiveTime`
  within 0.11%. Engines run in parallel, so engine times overlap and do not add up to the block.
- **HBM DMA** = the time of `DMA_*` instructions, whichever engine queued them (in HV1's dual
  block 48.3 of ScalarE's 53.7 ms of durations are DMA). **Collectives** = the union of the
  profile's collective operations.
- **Bottleneck** = the engine class with the most busy time; for two block kinds, each weighted by
  its executions per step. **Attention / Plan B addressable / cross-attention** = shares of block
  wall time on that engine; addressable = the self/joint attention at head dim 128 that
  `docs/plans/2026-11-13-mxfp8-attention.md` routes (`attn`, `attn1`; not LTX-2's `audio_attn1`,
  head dim 64, nor any cross-attention). **RoPE** = share on its own busiest engine.
- **Outside the blocks** = DiT step median (evidence doc) - sum over block NEFFs of executions
  per step x in-model median: embedders, final layer, host gaps, and the difference between the
  evidence run and the profiled run (two runs; section 6).
- **PSUM** is the accumulator memory TensorE writes into, not an engine. Its read-out runs on
  VectorE / ScalarE and is inside their times: pure copies (`COPY`, `CAST`: savable by fusion) and
  fused read-outs (compute that reads PSUM).
- **Unattributed** = instructions with no module path (`layer` "Unknown"), never folded into a
  component; `unattributed_by_source` names the source file and function they carry.

## 4. Reading

- **Linear MXFP8 moves the bottleneck from TensorE to VectorE** on Qwen-Image, FLUX, Wan 2.1 and
  Wan 2.2: TensorE time falls (0.59-0.85x per block, LTX-2 too; HunyuanVideo 0.92-0.93x) and
  VectorE time rises (1.10-1.27x, HunyuanVideo 1.02x; the added VectorE time sits in the quantized
  linears, q/k/v and MLP, not in attention), so VectorE, which also runs attention's mask and
  softmax, becomes the longest engine. MFU halves on the image models (46% -> 27% Qwen-Image,
  54% -> 28% FLUX) because TensorE is no longer the limit. LTX-2 stays TensorE-bound with VectorE
  close behind in MXFP8; HunyuanVideo's blocks stay VectorE-bound (section 4, last item).
- **Where TensorE's time goes is not the same on every model.** On Qwen-Image, FLUX and LTX-2 the
  drop is the quantized linears' (attention's TensorE time is unchanged). On Wan 2.1 it is mostly
  not: of TensorE's -14.18 ms, -10.02 ms is the attention component, whose kernel is bf16 in both
  arms (PV matmul 45.61 -> 36.04 ms, QK matmul 34.10 -> 31.52 ms), -3.58 ms is unattributed rows
  (Wan's FFN has no module path) and only -0.68 ms the attributed linears; Wan 2.2 repeats it. On
  HunyuanVideo's dual-stream block -11.13 of TensorE's -11.23 ms is attention. Why the bf16
  attention kernel's TensorE time drops in the MXFP8 build is not explained here. (HunyuanVideo's
  single-stream block's split is blurred by its component overlap, TensorE 1.16x.)
- **The block gain follows how much of the block is linear layers.** Blocks per step at 0.74x
  (LTX-2), 0.78x (Qwen-Image), 0.81x (FLUX), 0.97x (HunyuanVideo) and 0.98x (Wan 2.1, Wan 2.2),
  where attention holds about half the block or more on the bottleneck engine and stays bf16.
- **Attention is where the rest of the time is**, and almost all of it is what the MXFP8-attention
  plan can reach: 25% of the MXFP8 block on VectorE for Qwen-Image and FLUX, 52% for Wan, 56% and
  58% for HunyuanVideo's blocks, 73% of HunyuanVideo-1.5's bf16 block; for LTX-2 13% of its 17%
  (the rest is cross-attention and the audio stream). HunyuanVideo-1.5's MLP is entirely
  `unattributed`: its rows carry no module path, so it has no `mlp_*` component; by source
  (`unattributed_by_source`) it is GELU (`activations.py:GELU.gelu`, 4.1 ms on ScalarE) and the
  linears' forward (`ColumnParallelLinear.forward`, `RowParallelLinear.forward`).
- **The blocks are 93.8-99.6% of the DiT step**, and "outside the blocks" (0.4-6.2%) is an upper
  bound on the work outside them: it also holds the difference between the evidence run and the
  profiled run (section 6). FLUX's 9.9 ms (bf16) and 11.4 ms (MXFP8) are an upper bound that the
  1-step window cannot split: the profiled window runs from the first block's start to the last
  block's end (`system_profile.py`), so on a 1-step lane it holds only blocks (FLUX bf16: 0.08 ms
  of other NEFFs, 0.04 ms of host gaps) and cannot tell the embedders, final layer and host work
  apart from the run-to-run difference. That its profiled bf16 step, 209.9 ms, is below its
  blocks' 211.1 ms is no run difference either: the blocks are in-model medians times executions,
  the step is the window's own sum. Qwen-Image's 2-step window, which crosses a step boundary,
  shows 7.6 ms (bf16) and 6.6 ms (MXFP8) per step of other NEFFs and host gaps
  (`non_block_ms` + `host_gap_ms`).
- **Wan 2.2's block NEFF is Wan 2.1's** on every rank (same sha256), so its rows repeat Wan 2.1's
  within the replay spread; the steps differ only in step count and guidance.
- **HunyuanVideo's dual-stream block is HBM-DMA-bound, and most of that is attention glue.** Its
  execution-weighted blocks are VectorE-bound (60.7% and 63.8%), but the dual-stream block alone
  is HBM-DMA-bound in both arms: 244.9 ms (bf16) and 257.1 ms (MXFP8) of HBM DMA busy time in a
  458.2 and 451.0 ms block (VectorE 193.5 and 196.9 ms); the single-stream block is VectorE-bound

## 8. MXFP8 attention: gate G1, PV on `nc_matmul_mx`

## 9. attention_cte bf16 with the exp on ScalarE (`allow_vector_exp=False`)

Plan `docs/plans/2026-11-13-mxfp8-attention.md`, Task 4-pre (rulings BR18, BR19; 2026-11-14). At
wan_81f, shipped attention_cte is VectorE-bound (section 8: VectorE busy 62.0 of 63.5 ms, mostly the
MM1 PSUM evict and `exponential`) while ScalarE is nearly idle (1.6 ms). This section measures
nkilib's own switch for that, with no kernel edit.

**What the knob does** (`nkilib/core/attention/attention_cte.py` in
`/opt/aws_neuronx_venv_torch_neuronx_2_14_0/lib/python3.12/site-packages/`):

- `allow_vector_exp` (`:238`, docstring `:339`, default True) is one compile-time switch for the
  whole call. With it True on trn3 B1 (`is_trn3_b1()`), `:1361-1365` allocates
  `mm1_running_max_pos` (the un-negated row max that the VectorE `nisa.exponential` subtracts).
  With it False the buffer is never allocated.
- `_exp_impl` picks the engine per Q group (`:3323`): VectorE `nisa.exponential` when that buffer
  exists, unless some exp tile of the group has an odd element count (`:3353-3356`; the Vector and
  Scalar accumulators cannot be mixed within one chain). Otherwise it uses ScalarE
  `nisa.activation(op=exp, bias=-max, reduce add)` (`:3389`). So False puts the exp of every Q
  group on ScalarE; the row sum, P and everything after are the same.
- It does not move the MM1 evict (scale + row max, `TENSOR_SCALAR_CACHE_REDUCE`). That choice is
  hard-coded `use_scalar_row_max = False` (`:4075`), and its comment says `activate2` costs far more
  per element than `tensor_scalar_reduce`. The ScalarE placement code (`_row_max_on_scalar` `:3949`,
  `_emit_row_max_evict` `:3962`) is still there but switched off. It is not an argument, and
  nothing else in attention_cte's signature moves work between engines. It is not tried here.

**How it is called.** `scripts/mx_attention/probe.py --impl cte --cte-no-vector-exp`: the region of
section 7 (`ops_impl.attention.attention` as the models call it), with only the kernel call
changed. torch-neuronx's SDPA wrapper (`scaled_dot_product_attention_kernel` ->
`_compute_attention_nki`) does not forward `allow_vector_exp`. So on the unbounded path the probe
calls the same `wrapped_flash_fwd[(lnc,)]` directly, with `_compute_attention_nki`'s arguments
(`tp_q`, `tp_k`, `scale`, `causal_mask=False`, `cache_softmax=False`; the unit test compares them
with `_compute_attention_nki`'s own call) plus the knob, and does the `[B, H] -> [B*H]` reshape
outside the custom op. On the bounded path it uses `_load_flash_bounded_op`'s call plus the knob;
the fp32 q pre-scale, padding to 128 and `_head_chunk` are unchanged. Each new NEFF has a ScalarE
instruction stream that the shipped one lacks (flux: 76 KB ScalarE / 166 KB VectorE, against
none / 214 KB), and its outputs are not bit-identical to shipped, so the trace cache did not serve
the shipped kernel.

**QK^T row tiling, feasibility (Task 4 Step 1).** At head dim 128 an x4 MX operand of MM1 spans
32 partitions (128 d / 4 per packed element), so one `nc_matmul_mx` uses a quarter of the PE rows;
row tiling runs four in the four row quadrants. `scripts/mx_attention/row_tiling.py` is a minimal
kernel with the brief's ISA constraints applied verbatim: `tile_size=(32, 128)` (the stationary's 32
partitions set the row tile), stationary, moving and both scales at SBUF partition `start_row` =
0/32/64/96 with `tile_position=(start_row, 0)` (a quadrant's scales are its first 4 partitions),
stationary free size 128 (> 64), no `.indirect()`, and the four `[128, 512]` fp32 score tiles in
PSUM banks 0-3 (one bank each). Operands reach SBUF in the group-of-4 layout of the fork's `qk_perm`
(partition g byte j = head-dim index 4g + j; bf16 laid out on the host, `quantize_mx` on chip), and
each matmul group is issued 16 times, accumulating. Variants, each the same 33.5 M MACs per group:

- **keys**: one 128-query tile (stationary, the same in all four quadrants) x four 512-key tiles
  (moving, tile t in quadrant t): one of the fork's 2048-key large tiles.
- **queries**: four 128-query tiles (stationary, tile t in quadrant t) x one 512-key tile.
- **bf16**: the fork's MM1 today on the keys problem, four `nc_matmul`
  `[128 d, 128 q] x [128 d, 512 k]`; **bf16_one** (`4a/rowtile/bf16_one.py`, not in the repo): one
  `nc_matmul` over all 2048 keys (moving free 2048, four PSUM banks; it compiles).
- **untiled** (control): the keys operands' bytes in one full-array `nc_matmul_mx` (contraction over
  all 128 partitions: the four tiles summed).
- `--reload`: two copies of the stationary alternated per group, so it is loaded again every group
  (without it the compiler loads it once: 4 `LDWEIGHTS_MX` for 64 `MATMUL_MX`). The fork's bf16 MM1
  at wan_81f issues ~0.6 `LDWEIGHTS` per `MATMUL` (96,894 / 163,791, section 9's capture), between
  the two.

## 11. MXFP8 attention: gate G2, QK^T on `nc_matmul_mx` with row tiling

Plan `docs/plans/2026-11-13-mxfp8-attention.md`, Task 4b (brief Steps 2-5; rulings BR4, BR11, BR13,
BR15, BR19, BR20, BR21; 2026-11-14).

**The kernel** (commit `3d41b154`). `mx_attention_fwd(..., qk_mx=True)` puts MM1 on `nc_matmul_mx`,
with or without `pv_mx` (QK-only and QK+PV). Everything sits behind `ac.qk_mx`, and both flags off
stay attention_cte (bit for bit, below).

- **Q**, quantized per section and query group (`_mx_load_q`). One DMA reads the group's 128 rows
  four times into a `[128, 4 x 128]` bf16 tile, queries on partitions. Two stride-2 TensorE
  transposes of its fp32 view lay the four copies out in the four row quadrants: partition `32 t + g`,
  element `r` = query `r`'s head-dim indices `4g .. 4g + 3`, the `qk_perm` membership. A ScalarE copy
  takes them out of PSUM, and one `quantize_mx` writes the x4 data and scales. This is nkilib's
  `swizzle_quantize_mx` on explicit buffers, with all four quadrants in the same two transposes.
  A partial last group is zero-padded, so the stationary always has 128 columns (more than 64, the
  row-tiling rule). The kernel therefore needs no `S_q % 128` assertion, and wan_81f runs at
  32,760 queries on both arms. The PSUM test checks the `(32, 128)` stationary of every MM1 matmul.
- **K**, quantized once per section and 2048-key large tile (`_mx_load_k`), shared by every query
  group. Per 128-key chunk, one DMA brings rows `128 c ..` of the large tile's four 512-key tiles side
  by side, the same two transposes put tile t in quadrant t, then a copy and a `quantize_mx` into
  columns `128 c ..`. Keys past the end are zero.
- **MM1**, per (group, large tile): four `nc_matmul_mx` with `tile_position (32 t, 0)` and
  `tile_size (32, 128)`. The stationary is the group's Q in quadrant t (128 columns), the moving
  operand is tile t's K, and both scales sit at partition `32 t` (four key tiles share the PE array,
  ruling BR21). The scores stay fp32 in the large tile's four MM1 PSUM banks, and the evict
  (VectorE scale + row max) is unchanged.
- **PSUM.** Q's and K's transposes use bank 7, or bank 6 with `pv_mx` (P's transposes hold bank 7).
  MM1 then keeps five banks (0, 1, 2, 3, 5), and any four consecutive score tiles still land in
  distinct banks.
- **SBUF.** K's and Q's bf16 buffers give way to the MX ones. On all six shapes and every flag set,
  `exp_sb` keeps its two stages (checked at trace time, `4b/sim/sbuf_stages.json`).
- **Refused by neuronx-cc.** Replicating Q inside the transpose with a step-0 access pattern fails
  the BIR verifier: "RHS AP can only have one free dimension" (`NCC_INLA001`); the Python simulator
  accepts it (`4b/sim/q_step0/`). So the four copies come from the DMA, as four reads of the rows.
  A GpSimd copy of quadrant 0's quantized bytes to quadrants 1-3 compiles and is exact in BIRSim
  (`4b/sim/q_copy/`). It was not used, because it saves DMA only, and DMA does not bind (below).
- **K smoothing, on the torch side.** `smooth_keys` (`difflet/quant/mx_attention.py`) takes the
  mean over the keys in fp32 and rounds once. The probe's `use_fork(qk_mx=True)` applies it inside
  the compiled region. On the unbounded path that is right before the kernel; on the bounded path
  it comes before `_flash_attention_bounded` pads K with zeros, so the mean is over the call's real
  keys. That is where Task 5's op will do it. On the device, smoothing matches the CPU's (at flux, 1
  of 3.5 M elements differs, by 7e-9). Without smoothing the flux rel L2 is unchanged (2.06e-3).
  Its cost at flux is 32 µs of a 0.895 ms span (captures with and without it).
- **The quantizers' VectorE time** (`QUANTIZE_MX`, from the captures below). At wan_81f: 1.45 ms of
  46.2 ms VectorE busy, 10,880 instructions (Q 10,240, one per section and group; K 640, four per
  section and large tile). At flux_1024: 0.046 ms of 0.63 ms. The rest of the loaders' cost at wan:
  TensorE transposes 2.84 + 1.76 ms (`MATMUL` + `LDWEIGHTS`), ScalarE copies 1.84 ms, and Q's DMA
  5.09 ms (10,240 transfers of 128 KB).

**Membership on MM1's real loaders**, checked on device 10 (core 40, CPUs 16-23,112-119, `fuser`
idle) before any rel L2 was read. `scripts/mx_attention/membership.py` now runs `_mx_load_q` and
`_mx_load_k` themselves: one query group (`q_mm1`), 100 valid rows (`q_mm1_tail`), one large tile

Three things cancel MX's TensorE saving on MM1, and then exceed it:

1. **The four quadrant matmuls rarely run together inside the kernel.** Measured from the trace
   (`4b/mx_overlap.py`), their concurrency is 1.42 at wan and 2.2 at flux, against 5.9 in 4a's
   micro-kernel. One quadrant matmul takes 482 ns (median), longer than a full bf16 MM1 matmul (378
   ns, concurrency 1.18): its 512 moving columns set its time, not its 32 rows. So a large tile's
   MM1 costs 1,505 ns of TensorE (union), against 1,314 ns in bf16. The cause is PSUM: a large
   tile's four score tiles need four free banks at once, and with six MM1 banks rotating (five with
   `pv_mx`), the next large tile's matmuls wait for VectorE's evicts, 506 ns each. 4a's 0.32x came
   from four matmuls issued back to back into empty banks.
2. **Power throttling.** The PE runs under its 50% utilization limit for 45% of the QK-only span
   (average limit 77%). The rebalanced cte does so for 7% (96%), and 4a's pv_mx for 5% (97%). At flux
   the figures are 34% for QK-only against 18% for cte. Everything on the PE slows: bf16 MM2 alone
   goes from 28.7 to 32.3 ms of `MATMUL`.
3. **The layout's own cost.** Per (section, group) and (section, large tile): TensorE transposes
   4.6 ms, ScalarE copies 1.8 ms, VectorE `QUANTIZE_MX` 1.45 ms, and Q's DMA 5.1 ms.

TensorE becomes the binding engine (59.7 of 63.0 ms, 95% of the span), where the rebalanced cte was
balanced (TensorE 87%, P-transpose queue 84%, VectorE 82%; section 9). At the short shapes, MM1 is
a smaller share and VectorE, ScalarE and TensorE all sit near the span, so the added work and the
throttle cost 3-11%.

for QK+PV, and the long rows do not pass it for QK-only. The device's MX matmul explains every miss
