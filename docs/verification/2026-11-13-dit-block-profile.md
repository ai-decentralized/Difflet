# DiT block profile: seven models on trn3, bf16 against linear MXFP8

The user's request (2026-11-13): profile Qwen-Image, FLUX.1-dev, LTX-2, HunyuanVideo,
HunyuanVideo-1.5, Wan 2.1 and Wan 2.2 in PyTorch-native bf16 and with linear MXFP8 (HunyuanVideo-1.5
in bf16 only), find each one's bottleneck, and record where one DiT block spends its time

`docs/plans/2026-11-13-dit-block-profile.md`. Scripts: `scripts/profile_dit/` (README there).

**Page:** https://claude.ai/artifact/Em8C2DS8wBHcEPHAfk6rzy (a Data artifact: the datasets below
and `scripts/profile_dit/page/index.html`).

  the page's datasets and these tables. Nothing was rerun for step times or quality.

## 2. Definitions

- **Engine time** = busy time of one physical core: the union of its instruction intervals
  (overlapping pipelined instructions count once), `EVENT_SEMAPHORE*` waits excluded, then the mean
  over the two physical cores of the logical core. It reproduces the profile's `ActiveTime`
  within 0.11%. Engines run in parallel, so engine times overlap and do not add up to the block.
- **HBM DMA** = the time of `DMA_*` instructions, whichever engine queued them (in HV1's dual
  block 48.3 of ScalarE's 53.7 ms of durations are DMA). **Collectives** = the union of the
  profile's collective operations.
- **Bottleneck** = the engine class with the most busy time; for two block kinds, each weighted by
  its executions per step. **Attention / Plan B addressable / cross-attention** = shares of block
  wall time on that engine; addressable = the self/joint attention at head dim 128 that
  `docs/plans/2026-11-13-mxfp8-attention.md` routes (`attn`, `attn1`; not LTX-2's `audio_attn1`,
  head dim 64, nor any cross-attention). **RoPE** = share on its own busiest engine.
- **Outside the blocks** = DiT step median (evidence doc) - sum over block NEFFs of executions
  per step x in-model median: embedders, final layer, host gaps, and the difference between the
  evidence run and the profiled run (two runs; section 6).
- **PSUM** is the accumulator memory TensorE writes into, not an engine. Its read-out runs on
  VectorE / ScalarE and is inside their times: pure copies (`COPY`, `CAST`: savable by fusion) and
  fused read-outs (compute that reads PSUM).
- **Unattributed** = instructions with no module path (`layer` "Unknown"), never folded into a
  component; `unattributed_by_source` names the source file and function they carry.

## 4. Reading

- **Linear MXFP8 moves the bottleneck from TensorE to VectorE** on Qwen-Image, FLUX, Wan 2.1 and
  Wan 2.2: TensorE time falls (0.59-0.85x per block, LTX-2 too; HunyuanVideo 0.92-0.93x) and
  VectorE time rises (1.10-1.27x, HunyuanVideo 1.02x; the added VectorE time sits in the quantized
  linears, q/k/v and MLP, not in attention), so VectorE, which also runs attention's mask and
  softmax, becomes the longest engine. MFU halves on the image models (46% -> 27% Qwen-Image,
  54% -> 28% FLUX) because TensorE is no longer the limit. LTX-2 stays TensorE-bound with VectorE
  close behind in MXFP8; HunyuanVideo's blocks stay VectorE-bound (section 4, last item).
- **Where TensorE's time goes is not the same on every model.** On Qwen-Image, FLUX and LTX-2 the
  drop is the quantized linears' (attention's TensorE time is unchanged). On Wan 2.1 it is mostly
  not: of TensorE's -14.18 ms, -10.02 ms is the attention component, whose kernel is bf16 in both
  arms (PV matmul 45.61 -> 36.04 ms, QK matmul 34.10 -> 31.52 ms), -3.58 ms is unattributed rows
  (Wan's FFN has no module path) and only -0.68 ms the attributed linears; Wan 2.2 repeats it. On
  HunyuanVideo's dual-stream block -11.13 of TensorE's -11.23 ms is attention. Why the bf16
  attention kernel's TensorE time drops in the MXFP8 build is not explained here. (HunyuanVideo's
  single-stream block's split is blurred by its component overlap, TensorE 1.16x.)
- **The block gain follows how much of the block is linear layers.** Blocks per step at 0.74x
  (LTX-2), 0.78x (Qwen-Image), 0.81x (FLUX) and 0.98x (Wan 2.1, Wan 2.2), where attention holds
  about half the block on the bottleneck engine and stays bf16.
- **Attention is where the rest of the time is**, and almost all of it is what the MXFP8-attention
  plan can reach: 25% of the MXFP8 block on VectorE for Qwen-Image and FLUX, 52% for Wan, 56% and
  58% for HunyuanVideo's blocks, 73% of HunyuanVideo-1.5's bf16 block; for LTX-2 13% of its 17%
  (the rest is cross-attention and the audio stream).
- **The blocks are 93.8-99.6% of the DiT step**, and "outside the blocks" (0.4-6.2%) is an upper
  bound on the work outside them: it also holds the difference between the evidence run and the
  profiled run (section 6). FLUX's is that difference alone: its profiled bf16 step, 209.9 ms, is
  shorter than its blocks' 211.1 ms, and its MXFP8 one leaves 0.5 ms beside its blocks, against
  the 9.9 and 11.4 ms the evidence steps leave.
- **Wan 2.2's block NEFF is Wan 2.1's** on every rank (same sha256), so its rows repeat Wan 2.1's
  within the replay spread; the steps differ only in step count and guidance.
- **HunyuanVideo's dual-stream block is HBM-DMA-bound, and most of that is attention glue.** Its
  execution-weighted blocks are VectorE-bound (60.7% and 63.8%), but the dual-stream block alone
  is HBM-DMA-bound in both arms: 244.9 ms (bf16) and 257.1 ms (MXFP8) of HBM DMA busy time in a
  458.2 and 451.0 ms block (VectorE 193.5 and 196.9 ms); the single-stream block is VectorE-bound
  (79%, 84%). Of that HBM DMA time, 179.7 and 192.1 ms (73% and 75%) is `attn_glue`. In both arms
  nearly all of it is two HLO slices of one padded tensor (bf16 `slice.415` 92.2 ms and `slice.409`
  86.5 ms, both of `pad.406`; MXFP8 `slice.414` 98.5 and `slice.420` 93.1 ms, both of `pad.411`;
  unions per op from the execution-4 parquet), in the attention module beside two custom calls
  whose outputs are concatenated (`concatenate.421` of `custom-call.414` and `.420`; MXFP8
  `concatenate.426`). The only source those rows name is `ops_impl/attention.py:204`, the line
  that runs the bounded attention kernel per head chunk on q, k and v padded to multiples of 128.
  Which tensor `pad.406` / `pad.411` is, and why its two slices take this long, is not resolved
  here. The single-stream block has 0.1-0.3 ms of `attn_glue` HBM DMA.

## 6. Not measured

- Step times were not rerun: they are the evidence doc's (sections 7f and 11 on host A, section 12
  on host B). The profiled run's own step (`profile_step_ms`, its loop window from the first block
  to the last, so without the work before the first block and after the last) is 0.4-6.0% shorter
  than the evidence step, over 1% in 8 of 13 configurations (the overview's "profiled run's step"
  column): FLUX -5.0% and -6.0%, Qwen-Image MXFP8 -1.8%, LTX-2 -1.2% and -1.0%, Wan 2.1 MXFP8
  -1.2%, Wan 2.2 -1.0% and -1.2%. "Outside the blocks" therefore holds that run-to-run
  difference too (section 4).
- HunyuanVideo-1.5 has no MXFP8 arm (no MXFP8 default since 2026-11-09); its quality rows are the
  G1 + G3 override's.
- Wan 2.2's low-noise expert (`--steps 1` runs the high-noise one; both are the same block NEFF
  shape).
- Quality at the profiled shape exists only for FLUX, Qwen-Image and LTX-2 (CPU fp32 and device
  bf16), Wan and HunyuanVideo-1.5 at the official settings (device bf16, section 12) and
  HunyuanVideo at 544x960x129 (device bf16, 7f); no CPU fp32 reference at Wan 81 frames,
  HunyuanVideo 544x960x129 or HunyuanVideo-1.5 121 frames.
- The work outside the block NEFFs is a remainder, not broken down. Every NEFF carries the
  profiler's "missing compiler metrics" warning. HunyuanVideo is captured without DGE
  notifications (its blocks overflow the notification queue), so its DMA attribution lacks the DGE
  metadata. All four HunyuanVideo breakdowns also carry the profiler's warning "Notifications were
  dropped from buffer types EVENT. This can cause corresponding data to be incorrect"; their engine
  unions still match the profile's `ActiveTime` within 0.011% (bf16 dual TensorE 168.14 against
  168.12 ms).
