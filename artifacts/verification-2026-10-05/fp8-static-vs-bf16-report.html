FP8 Static vs bf16 on Trainium

What FP8 W8A8 static-scale quantization of the DiT linears buys over the unquantized bf16 path on trn2 at tensor parallelism 4: per-step latency, end-to-end latency, compile cost and render quality, across six models.

Summary

FP8 static quantizes the attention q/k/v/out and FFN in/out linears of each DiT to FP8 e4m3 with per-tensor weight scales and one calibrated activation scale per layer; everything else stays bf16. Measured against the same bf16 model, same prompt, seed and shape, on trn2.3xlarge:

0.83–0.97×
DiT step vs bf16 on Wan 2.1, HunyuanVideo, Qwen-Image, FLUX (LTX-2 is the exception at 1.035×)
−11 to −28 %
cold end-to-end latency: FP8 weights are half the bytes to read and shard
27–35 dB
PSNR of the FP8 render against the bf16 render; LPIPS 0.03–0.10
478 s
Wan 2.1 FP8 compile (transformer only) against 7,879 s for a full bf16 build

The step-time picture changed on 2026-10-05. Until then FP8 was 2–4 % slower than bf16 on Wan while winning everywhere else. The cause was not quantization: Wan's transformer stage ran without NEURON_RT_VIRTUAL_CORE_SIZE=2, which the other models already had. With that and --vectorize-strided-dma as the new Wan default, both arms get 15 % faster and FP8 ends 2.7 % ahead of bf16. Section 5 has the detail.

  • Joint-attention models gain the most. HunyuanVideo −17 %, Qwen-Image −13 %, FLUX −5 %. Their token rows (4096, 10240, 1024, 512) are 16-aligned, and they already ran VC2.
  • Wan 2.1 flipped from +2.4 % to −2.7 % once its transformer stage ran VC2 with strided DMA. Numerics are bit-identical to the old graph; only the schedule changed.

Its 2310 video rows are not 16-aligned and its audio stream adds ~51-row FP8 matmuls where the quantize chain dominates. FP8 still buys LTX-2 the cold-start win below.

The cold gain is the weight bytes: Wan 2.1's transformer shrinks from 57.2 GB to 15.0 GB on disk, and the first run has to read and shard whatever is there. Warm runs already have the weights in the page cache, so the warm difference is essentially the DiT step difference times the step count.

With the 2026-10-05 Wan defaults (host VAE, so not directly comparable with the table): warm e2e bf16 89.5 s, FP8 89.3 s; cold 179.2 s vs 86.2 s.

Runs are bit-deterministic: two FP8 runs of the same seed give identical latents, and so do two bf16 runs. The scheduling levers of 2026-10-05 (VC2, strided DMA, bf16-domain quantize) change none of the FP8 values: three FP8 arms compared against one bf16 render gave the same latent cosine to six digits.

For reference, the retired weight-only FP8 mode (FP8 weights dequantized to bf16, bf16 matmuls) measured 24.6 dB PSNR on Wan 2.1 at bf16 step parity (563 ms), 9 dB below W8A8 static, and was removed on 2026-10-03.

The VC2 finding: why Wan looked like the loser

An instruction-level trace of the Wan 2.1 FP8 step showed the scalar engine 63 ms busier than bf16 (the fp32 dequantize and the ×1/scale) and the tensor engine waiting on it; 79 % of the FP8 dots ran single-row because Wan's 4680 token rows tile as 12 × 390 and 390 is not a multiple of 16. We screened runtime and compiler levers on a 2-block probe and confirmed the winners on the full 20-step model, same host, same day:

  • VC2 is the lever (−14 % alone on the probe); strided DMA adds 1–2 % on top for Wan and nothing measurable on HunyuanVideo (726.0 → 722.6 ms), Qwen-Image (362.2 → 360.0 ms) or FLUX (4.31 → 4.39 it/s), which already ran VC2. Those three keep strided DMA off.
  • LTX-2 gets both switches too: bf16 459.7 → 413.7 ms. Its FP8 graph gains less (440.3 → 428.4 ms), which is why it is the one model where FP8 is now slower than bf16.
  • What did not help: bf16-domain quantize and dequantize (neutral, identical FP8 output), padding the token sequence to 128/256/512 rows for double-row matmuls (15–23 % slower for bf16 and FP8 alike), the compiler's internal matmul flags (not reachable from the 2.26 command line), a hand-written NKI FP8 linear kernel (17–30 % slower: the custom-call boundary costs more fusion than the fused epilogue saves).

Method and evidence

  • Hardware and toolchain. trn2.3xlarge, one Trainium2 chip as 4 logical NeuronCores, tensor parallelism 4, neuronx-cc 2.26.6360, NxD 0.19, nki 0.5.0, torch 2.9.1. Host idle during every measurement; no two compiles overlap.
  • DiT step is the device-synced wall time per denoising step from the generate log, step 0 excluded, median of 19. End to end is the difflet generate wall clock. Two runs per arm; run 0 cold, run 1 warm.
  • FP8 arms use --quant fp8 --quant-calibration <model>.json; the calibration is a CPU pass over the real 20-step loop at the benchmark shape.
  • Quality compares the FP8 render with the bf16 render of the same prompt and seed (scripts/ptq_compare_outputs.py: PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS-alex, latent cosine and SNR). Single-seed latent SNR alone is not a usable gate: CPU bf16 vs device bf16 already differs by 14 dB after 20 steps.
  • Evidence in the repo (branch quantization): artifacts/verification-2026-10-05/fp8-ab/ (Wan A/Bs), artifacts/verification-2026-10-05/model-screen/ (per-model CLI runs), artifacts/verification-2026-10-05/fp8-screen/ (2-block screen), docs/verification/2026-10-03-ptq-fp8-static-fanout.md and docs/verification/2026-10-05-fp8-step-levers.md.
